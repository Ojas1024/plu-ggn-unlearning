Forgetting the Pipeline Too

When a patient withdraws consent, a hospital model should behave as if that patient was never in the training data. Most unlearning methods update only the model weights, but tabular models also learn their preprocessing (imputation means, scaling, target encodings) from the data. This project measures what that costs, and fixes it.

Read the paper (PDF, 4 pages) Try it on your own data Source code

The short version

A frozen preprocessor remembers the deleted patients. Deleting the 3% heaviest hospital users shrinks the scale of number_inpatient from 1.20 to 0.78, but a frozen pipeline keeps 1.20.
Standard unlearning moves the weights the wrong way. A standard influence (Newton) update ends further from the retrained weights than doing nothing (5 out of 5 seeds), even though its predictions improve.
PLU-GGN fixes both. An exact O(|Df|) downdate of the preprocessor, plus a gradient term for the changed coordinates. Weight error drops 26× with one step and to ~10−5 with two, about 5× faster than retraining.
A tempting privacy result didn't hold up. A membership audit that looked like 0.98 AUC was really measuring how extreme a patient is. Against equally extreme controls it is ~0.56.
